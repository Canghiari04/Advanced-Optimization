Problem
- Schedule EV charging + V2G discharging in a microgrid over 1 day (24 x 1 h)
- Goal: minimise total electricity cost
- Price rises with load: g(z) = k0 + k1*z
- Cost per hour = price integrated from base load to total load (quadratic)
- Result: charge in low-demand hours, discharge at peaks (valley filling)

Variables
- x_mi = power of EV m in hour i (+ charge, - discharge)
- z_i = total load in hour i
- 200 EVs x 24 h + 24 = 4824 variables

Constraints
- (5b) total load = base load + EV power
- (5c) battery between empty and full every hour
- (5d) EV leaves with >= 90% charge
- (5e) charging-only EVs: 0 <= x <= Pmax
- (5f) V2G EVs: -Pmax <= x <= Pmax

Method
- Quadratic objective + linear constraints = convex QP
- Solved with CVX (interior point)
- Global scheme: knows whole day, solved once -> benchmark, not practical
- Local scheme: per group, re-solved every hour with forecasts (like MPC) -> practical
- Extension: battery wear cost (beta*x^2 + eta*dx^2), still convex

Results (200 EVs, 16 kWh, Pmax = 5 kW)
- Global 237.26 C$, Local 240.52 C$, Equal allocation 261.88 C$
- Savings 9.4% (global), 8.2% (local)
- Local only 1.4% worse than global
- Gap shrinks with bigger groups + better forecasts

Strengths
- Clear, convex, globally optimal
- Local scheme is scalable and realistic

Weaknesses
- Groups don't coordinate -> new peaks with small groups
- No battery losses; V2G free without wear term
- Only system cost, no user fairness
- No grid/feeder limits
- Assumes known departure times
- Single scenario, no statistics

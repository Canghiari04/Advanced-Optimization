Selected project: Option A

Charging electric vehicles (EVs) places new demands on electricity networks, while flexible charging and, where appropriate, vehicle-to-grid operation offer opportunities to manage charging costs and network peaks. In this option, you will examine a published optimization model, reproduce numerical results, and develop a meaningful extension.

Reference paper. Y. He, B. Venkatesh, and L. Guan, “Optimal scheduling for charging and discharging of electric vehicles,” IEEE Transactions on Smart Grid, vol. 3, no. 3, pp. 1095–1105, 2012.

A1. Understand the reference model. Read the paper and prepare a critical summary (maximum one page). Explain the objectives, decision variables, principal constraints, assumptions, and optimization method.


A2. Reproduce the implementation. Study and run the CVX code provided with the course materials. Relate
the code to the mathematical formulation and reproduce at least one numerical result. Document discrepancies or technical difficulties.
A3. Perform a sensitivity study. Vary at least two relevant parameters (e.g., electricity prices, charging limits,battery capacity, or EV availability). Present and interpret the changes in the schedules and objective value.
A4. Develop a model extension. Introduce a meaningful change, such as feeder-capacity limits, renewable- energy coordination, a new tariff design, or user-preference constraints. Formulate the revised objective and
constraints.
A5. Solve and evaluate the extension. Implement an appropriate optimization method, compare the extended and original models, and discuss feasibility, trade-offs, limitations, and further improvements.
